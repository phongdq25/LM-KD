# Concepts & Theoretical Foundations

Knowledge Distillation (KD) for Large Language Models extends far beyond traditional classification KD (Hinton et al., 2015). In autoregressive language generation, distilling knowledge involves sequential state spaces, dynamic sequence generation, tokenizer vocabulary mismatches, and multi-step reasoning trajectories.

This section provides the formal theoretical framework underlying the LM-KD toolkit.

---

## Conceptual Overview

Unlike classification tasks where inputs are static images and targets are categorical labels, knowledge transfer in LLMs is characterized by two distinct, coupled components at every training step $t$:

$$\mathcal{L}_t(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{s \sim d_t} \left[ \ell \left( P_\theta(\cdot \mid s),\, \tau_{T,t}(s) \right) \right]$$

Where:
1. **$d_t$ (The Context Distribution):** The distribution over sequential prefix states $s = (x, y_{<j})$. Where does the input context come from? Is it from a human dataset, generated by the teacher, rolled out by the student's current policy, or sampled from a lagged replay buffer?
2. **$\tau_{T,t}(s)$ (The Teacher Guidance Signal):** The supervisory signal provided by the teacher on context $s$. Is it full next-token logits, top-$K$ probabilities, hidden states, attention maps, or sequence-level scalar rewards?
3. **$\ell(\cdot, \cdot)$ (The Divergence Criterion):** The mathematical loss function measuring disagreement between the student's predictive distribution $P_\theta$ and the teacher's signal $\tau_T$ (e.g., Forward KL, Reverse KL, Jensen-Shannon, Total Variation, or Sinkhorn distance).
